01 — The problem

Negative obstacles are invisible at any budget

Ditches, craters and washouts are detected by the absence of laser returns. A narrow trench is not entered by beams — it is stepped over. Measured on 40 full scans, every ray, no budget limit:

02 — The solution

A ditch leaves a gap that geometry cannot explain

Two adjacent beams that both return land a predictable distance apart on level ground. If the far one lands much further out, the surface between them dropped away.

The comparison must be against what geometry predicts at that range — ground spacing grows quadratically, so a fixed threshold flags flat ground seven times over.

anomaly = measured gap ÷ predicted gap(r)

03 — Technical approach

What the geometry also tells you: how fast you may drive

A bump of height h at range r needs angular spacing h/r. A ditch of width w needs w·h/r² — quadratically tighter. A safety floor sized for bumps is comfortably met while ditches stay invisible.

Which lidar does this run on?

  • Steerable (MEMS/OPA) — emits only budgeted rays; saves laser energy.
  • Fixed-pattern (Velodyne, Ouster) — every ray fires; the budget selects what is processed. Saves bandwidth, memory and compute — not laser power.
  • Replay — the evaluation harness.

What we deliberately do not build

  • SLAM — we consume ego-motion, we do not estimate it
  • Sensor fusion — lidar only, so the measurement is not confounded
  • Path planning, dashboards, cloud

05 — Prior art, and what is ours

This field exists. We read it first.

Adaptive lidar sampling is established

Pittaluga et al., Towards a MEMS-based Adaptive LIDAR, NEC Labs (arXiv 2003.09545) · Adaptive LiDAR Scanning: Harnessing Temporal Cues (arXiv 2508.01562, 2025) · AEye iDAR / 4Sight, US 11675053 / 11782136 / 11860313 · Fast Adaptive Scene Sampling for Single-Photon 3D Lidar

So is negative-obstacle detection

Larson & Trivedi, Lidar Based Off-road Negative Obstacle Detection, DTIC ADA561293 · Negative Obstacle Detection Using Sparse 3D LiDAR, IEEE ROBIO 2024 · RELLIS-3D, ICRA 2021

Honest limitations

  • Rolling terrain raises 70 false flags per scan at threshold 2.0; crest occlusions
  • Results are on a controlled testbed with exact ground truth, not yet RELLIS-3D
  • Smart allocation does not beat plain decimation on whole-map recall — it wins only on warning distance
What is ours

Every published adaptive-lidar result targets urban driving, where negative obstacles barely exist. Every negative-obstacle result assumes dense uniform scanning.

The two fields have never been put in the same room.

We put them there, and found that adaptive sampling creates exactly the absences that hide a ditch — then derived a detector from the same geometry that causes the problem.
