# Smart India Hackathon 2026 — Research Brief

> Compiled 5 September 2026. Verify every date and rule against **sih.gov.in** and your
> college SPOC before acting — the portal is the source of record, everything below is
> assembled from public reporting and community trackers.

---

## 1. What SIH actually is

Smart India Hackathon is a nationwide hackathon run by the **Ministry of Education's
Innovation Cell (MIC)** together with **AICTE**. It has run since 2017; **SIH 2026 is the
9th edition**. The 8th edition (SIH 2025) drew **over 72,000 idea submissions**.

The structure that matters:

- **Problem statements are supplied by real customers** — central ministries, government
  departments, PSUs, state governments and industry partners. You are not inventing a
  problem; you are solving a named organisation's stated problem, and that organisation
  sits on your jury.
- **Two editions:**
  - **Software** — 36-hour continuous grand finale.
  - **Hardware** — 5-day grand finale (build + test + demo).
- **Nodal centres** — the finale is distributed across ~50 centres nationwide, each
  hosting a set of problem statements. You compete against the other teams assigned to
  *your* problem statement, not against all 72,000.
- **Post-event pathway** — IP stays with the students; the sponsoring ministry gets
  lifetime free access. Winners are routed toward incubation, internships and, in some
  cases, actual deployment (target timeline 6–12 months).

**Key consequence:** SIH is not a "build something cool" hackathon. It is a
**procurement-shaped competition**. The winning move is to look like the vendor the
ministry would actually hire.

### The two deadlines that actually kill teams

1. **Your college's internal hackathon date** — set by *your* SPOC, not by AICTE, and
   almost always **weeks earlier than 30 September**. If your college's internal round is
   mid-September, your real deadline is ~10 days away, not 25.
2. **30 September 2026** — the SPOC submits nominated teams' ideas on the national portal.
   Only teams that cleared the internal hackathon can be nominated.

> **Action item, today:** find out (a) whether your college registered a SPOC before
> 31 July, (b) the internal hackathon date, (c) how many teams your college may nominate.
> If your college has no registered SPOC, you cannot enter through it — that has to be
> resolved first and everything else is moot.

## 3. Team rules

- **Exactly 6 student members**, team leader included.
- **At least 1 female member** — mandatory, non-negotiable.
- **All 6 from the same college.** Inter-college teams are not permitted.
- **2 mentors** accompany the team at the finale.
- Only teams **selected through the institution's internal hackathon** may be nominated.
- **Nomination quota varies by institution** (reported examples range from ~25 to ~45
  teams). Confirm with your SPOC; do not assume.

## 5. How you are scored

### Idea / screening stage

Assessed on: **novelty (originality), complexity, clarity and detail in the prescribed
format, feasibility, practicability, sustainability, scale of impact, user experience,
and potential for future development.**

A commonly used breakdown at internal and screening level:

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| Problem Statement understanding | 10 |
| Novelty of the Idea | 10 |
| Complexity | 10 |
| Clarity | 10 |
| Execution / feasibility | 10 |

### Grand finale

- The finale rubric is usually framed as five axes: **Innovation, Invention/Novelty,
  Technical Feasibility, Impact & Benefits, Architecture.**
- Teams are scored **1–20 per criterion per round**, weighted and combined into a
  **final score out of 100**.
- **~3 jury members** per team, drawn from the sponsoring organisation and industry.
- **Four evaluation stages.** The first three are mentoring/critique rounds — judges
  probe the idea, challenge it, and help you improve it. The fourth is the **"power
  round"**: the final pitch. **The power round is make-or-break.**

**Implication:** rounds 1–3 are not just assessments, they are free consulting from your
actual customer. Teams that treat early-round criticism as feedback to implement — and
visibly ship it by the next round — climb. Teams that defend their original design sink.

## 6. The idea submission (PPT)

- **Maximum 6 slides, submitted as PDF.**
- **Always use the official template from the portal** if one is published — a
  non-conforming deck can be rejected on format alone.

Standard slide structure:

1. **Title** — team name, PS ID, PS title, organisation, theme, category
2. **Problem statement / idea overview**
3. **Proposed solution & approach**
4. **Technical approach — feasibility & tech stack**
5. **Impact & benefits**
6. **Research & references** (+ team details)

Deck failure modes that get flagged repeatedly: **wall-of-text slides with no visuals,
buzzword-stuffed "AI-powered" claims with no mechanism, vague problem framing, and a
hand-waved impact section with no numbers.**

## 7. Why teams lose

Ordered roughly by how many teams each one takes out.

1. **Administrative elimination.** Reported that **over 80% of teams are eliminated before
   writing a line of code** — missed SPOC dates, invalid 6-person team composition, or
   choosing a problem statement that was withdrawn/dead.
2. **Misreading the problem statement.** Teams solve the problem they *imagined* rather
   than the one written. Every clause in the statement is a scored requirement. Drift
   from it and a technically excellent solution still loses.
3. **Feature bloat.** Time spent on extras while the core requirement stays half-built.
4. **Documentation and deck quality.** Teams with working code get cut at the slides
   stage because the submission was incomplete or unclear.
5. **No working prototype.** Judges consistently prefer a rough working thing over a
   polished theoretical thing.
6. **Integration collapse at the finale.** The most-cited finale failure: frontend and
   backend that were never integrated until hour 30.
7. **Demo tech failure.** "Teams lose rounds to laptops, not to logic" — no offline
   fallback, no seeded demo data, live-network dependency.

## 8. What winners actually do

Drawn from winner write-ups across SIH 2022–2025.

**Before the hackathon**

- **Do the domain research nobody else does.** Read the full statement, then find the
  sponsoring ministry's *own* reports, circulars and standards on that subject and
  understand *why* the problem exists and what they've already tried. This is repeatedly
  named as the single biggest differentiator, and almost no team does it.
- **Talk to teams that reached the finale and lost**, not only to winners — the failure
  modes are more instructive.
- **Build a team with defined, non-overlapping roles** — ML, backend, frontend, hardware,
  design, and a presenter/PM. Balanced teams with good communication beat
  higher-skilled but poorly-coordinated ones. Mix seniors and juniors.

**The build**

- **Working prototype over theory** — every time. Even basic beats slideware.
- **Integrate early and continuously.** Never leave FE↔BE integration to the last day.
- **Meaningful use of AI/ML, blockchain, IoT, cloud** — as the mechanism that solves the
  problem, not as decoration. Judges from the sponsoring org can tell the difference.
- **Real-world testing with real data.** The NALCO winner won on *accuracy and
  scalability* demonstrated to industry evaluators. The Solar Masters team won by
  demonstrating a measured **20% efficiency gain** over a fixed panel.
- **Deploy it.** A live URL / running device beats localhost.

**At the finale**

- **Implement the mentors' feedback between rounds and show it.**
- **Rehearse the power round** as a separate deliverable from the build.
- **Bring offline fallbacks** — recorded demo video, seeded local database, no reliance
  on venue wifi.

### Examples of what a winning project looks like

| Project | Why it won |
|---|---|
| AI train traffic control for max section throughput (Ministry of Railways) — Team NYX, SIH 2025 winner | Hard optimisation problem, direct operational metric (throughput) |
| AYUSH NAMASTE ↔ ICD-11 bridging API for EMR compliance (Ministry of AYUSH) — Team Passengers, SIH 2025 winner | Narrow, standards-driven, unambiguously useful to the ministry |
| Low-cost portable jute ribboning machine (Min. of Agriculture) — Team Caffeinated Coders, SIH 2025 Hardware winner, ₹1.5L | Physical device, cost-constrained, field-testable |
| NALCO aluminium wire-rod property optimisation (Team Mad Astra, IIIT Bhopal, SIH 2024) | ML with demonstrated accuracy + scalability on the sponsor's real process |
| Single-axis solar tracker in MATLAB/Simulink/Simscape (Solar Masters, SIH 2024) | Quantified 20% efficiency gain; 6 of 106 teams reached the finale |
| Legal case tracking for Ministry of Law & Justice | Full-stack + ML, beat 400+ submissions on integration quality |

**The common thread:** a *specific, measurable improvement* to a *named organisation's
existing process*, demonstrated live.

## 9. Prizes and what winning gets you

- **₹1 lakh** per winning team, per problem statement (software). Hardware winners were
  reported at **₹1.5 lakh** in 2025.
- **IP remains with the students.** The sponsoring ministry gets lifetime free access —
  deliberately structured so startups can be built on SIH solutions.
- Incubation placements, corporate innovation labs, internships, scholarships.
- Ministry of Education commits to supporting further development and deployment;
  intended timeline 6–12 months. In practice, government pace is slow and some team
  members graduate before hearing back — treat implementation as a bonus, not a plan.

## 10. Strategy — how to play this

**Pick the problem statement like a bet, not like a preference.**

Score every candidate statement on:

1. **Can we build a *demonstrable* version in the time we have?** If the honest answer is
   "we'd have a mockup", drop it.
2. **Do we have or can we get real data?** A statement with no accessible dataset is a
   trap unless you can generate/simulate defensibly.
3. **Is there a measurable metric we can move?** Throughput, accuracy, cost, latency,
   efficiency, time-saved. Statements that admit a number are winnable; statements that
   only admit "a platform" are a knife fight on presentation quality.
4. **How contested is it?** Smart Automation has 55 statements and attracts the most
   generic entries. Narrow, technical, unglamorous statements (standards compliance,
   metrology, signal processing, geospatial correspondence) have fewer serious teams and
   more room to look like the obvious winner.
5. **Does someone on the team have a genuine edge?** Prior domain exposure, a relevant
   project, a contact in the sector.
6. **Is the sponsoring org's material accessible?** If you can read their reports and
   standards, you can out-research everyone else on that statement.

**Then: build for the demo, not for the repo.** The deliverable that gets scored is a
live demonstration to a domain expert, plus a 6-slide PDF. Architect backwards from that.
